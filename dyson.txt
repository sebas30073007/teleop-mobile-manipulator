Nombre del proyecto: Remote Hands
Resume tu proyecto en una frase: Remote Hands is a mixed-reality teleoperated robot that lets people do warehouse tasks from anywhere, making physical logistics work accessible to people with lower-limb mobility disabilities.
Para qué sirve: Remote Hands helps a seated operator navigate, see, pick and place objects in a arehouse through a mobile robot with an arm. It turns demanding logistics tasks into remote work for people who can use their hands and head, but have limited leg mobility.
Qué te inspiró: The idea came from two problems that rarely meet in the same solution. Warehouses are full of repetitive but still very human tasks: moving through aisles, checking shelves, picking objects and adapting when something changes. Full automation struggles in these shared spaces. At the same time, many people with lower-limb mobility disabilities are excluded from physical jobs, even when they have the skills and attention to do them. I wanted to turn a warehouse task into something that could be done seated, from another place, using a robot as a remote body. The goal was not to replace workers, but to open jobs usually closed to them.
Cómo funciona: The operator wears a Meta Quest headset and sees the robot’s camera feed inside a mixed-reality interface. From the same view, they can drive the mobile base, check robot telemetry, read a LiDAR map and move the arm with simple controls. The robot combines a wheeled platform, a three-axis steel arm and a rack-and-pinion gripper to pick and place real objects. A computer on the robot receives commands over WiFi and separates control, video, telemetry and spatial data into independent channels, so heavy sensor streams do not delay movement commands. Embedded ESP32-C3 modules drive the base through custom H-bridge boards, while stepper drivers move the arm joints and another controller operates the gripper. Safety was built into the system: the firmware starts disarmed, the arm only moves after an explicit command, a watchdog stops the robot if communication is lost, and an emergency stop button cuts motion immediately.
Proceso de diseño: We followed a simulation-first, build-test-redesign process because the project combined electronics, mechanics, software and human interaction. For the motor driver, every circuit was first tested in Falstad, then built on a protoboard and only later converted into a two-layer PCB in KiCad. The board was fabricated, hand soldered and improved after early failures, adding decisions such as optocoupler isolation between logic and power and firmware that always boots in a safe, disarmed state. The mechanical arm followed the same logic in Autodesk Inventor. We designed the parts, laser-cut them in steel sheet, assembled the structure and redesigned the pieces that did not behave well in real life. Some L-shaped brackets went through several versions; when bending the steel was not reliable, we split parts and bolted them instead. Testing also showed that some sheet-metal sections flexed under load, so we reinforced the critical riser with doubled material. The interface was redesigned after testing what felt natural inside the headset: we moved from a cluttered layout to three clear panels with video, map and controls. Finally, we validated communication with thousands of latency probes across sensor loads to make sure the robot stayed responsive.
Qué lo hace diferente: Remote Hands is different because it is designed as an accessible work tool, not only as a research robot. Many teleoperation systems use expensive platforms and assume the wireless link will work. We focused on making the whole system understandable, affordable and responsive. The robot uses a rehabilitated mobile base, laser-cut steel links, catalog motors and custom open-hardware motor drivers instead of a six-figure platform. Its communication layer separates commands, video, telemetry and spatial data, so the operator keeps control even when the robot is streaming heavy sensor information. The interface is also built around the person: the operator remains seated and uses only head and hand movement to navigate, see and manipulate objects. The goal is not full autonomy or worker replacement, but a practical bridge between human judgment and robotic reach.
Planes para el futuro: Next, we want to complete full pick-and-place testing with the integrated arm and run user trials with seated operators, including people with lower-limb mobility disabilities. We will measure task time, comfort, control effort and safety. Technically, we plan to improve the spatial data compression, harden the system against WiFi interference and test one operator supervising more than one robot. The long-term goal is to pilot Remote Hands in a local micro-fulfillment warehouse in Mexico City and turn the prototype into a reproducible, low-cost platform for accessible remote logistics work.
